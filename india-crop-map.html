A 10 m crop map of India from street-level imagery and vision-language-model labels

Gemini 3.1 Pro annotates street-level imagery in place of field surveys, and its labels supervise a classifier on foundation-model embeddings to map India at 10 m.

J. Laguarta Soler, S. Cheng, E. Liao, S. Wang · first author

Skills: VLM-as-annotator, Cross-modal distillation, Foundation-model embeddings, Country-scale inference

Interactive demo (five-crop version)

My role

I built the annotation pipeline (image filtering, VLM labelling, projection of each label onto the observed field), the Sentinel-1/2 time-series CNN baselines, and the classifier on frozen AlphaEarth embeddings that produces the national map.

Problem

Crop-type mapping is bottlenecked by ground labels: the largest previous Indian set, WBCrop, has 45,616 points. We test whether a vision-language model labelling street-level imagery can supply that supervision at national scale, with no field surveys. The project scales up the approach of our Thailand crop map (AAAI 2024).

Method

Gemini 3.1 Pro labels the crop in street-level images of growing fields; each label is projected onto its field. Training on these labels distils the VLM's street-level predictions into a lightweight classifier on frozen AlphaEarth embeddings, which predicts one of eight classes (seven crops and non-cropland) at every 10 m pixel for the 2023 Kharif season.

Delivery

National inference uses the embedding classifier on Google Earth Engine and finishes in under four hours. A Sentinel-1/2 time-series CNN is more accurate on the same test set but would need an estimated one to two years of national inference, so the national map trades that accuracy for throughput.

Limitations

  • Millets have the weakest district agreement (r = 0.60).
  • Accuracy drops in regions far from any training label.
  • Nationally, cotton is over-mapped and pulses under-mapped relative to official area statistics.
