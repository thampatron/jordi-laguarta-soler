A 10 m crop map of India from street-level imagery and vision-language-model labels

Supervised by 728,490 vision-language-model annotations of street-level imagery, with no field surveys: 77.1% overall accuracy on 17,247 expert-labelled test points.

J. Laguarta Soler, S. Cheng, E. Liao, S. Wang · first author

Interactive demo (five-crop version)

My role

I built the annotation pipeline (image filtering, VLM labelling, projection of each label onto the observed field), the Sentinel-1/2 time-series CNN baselines, and the classifier on frozen AlphaEarth embeddings that produces the national map.

Problem

Crop-type mapping is bottlenecked by ground labels: the largest previous Indian set, WBCrop, has 45,616 points.

Method

Gemini 3.1 Pro serves as the annotator: it labels the crop in street-level images of growing fields, and each label is projected onto the observed field, giving 728,490 labels across all 737 agricultural districts. The labels supervise a lightweight classifier on frozen AlphaEarth embeddings, which predicts one of eight classes (seven crops and non-cropland) at every 10 m pixel for the 2023 Kharif season.

National inference time

Crop labels, log scale: 16× WBCrop, ~100× AgriFieldNet

Delivery

National inference uses the embedding classifier on Google Earth Engine and finishes in under four hours. A Sentinel-1/2 time-series CNN is more accurate on the same test set (macro F1 0.78) but would need an estimated one to two years of national inference, so the deployed model trades that accuracy for throughput.

Limitations

  • Millets have the weakest district agreement (r = 0.60); intercropping makes millets and pulses hard to separate at 10 m.
  • Accuracy drops in regions far from any training label.
  • Nationally, cotton is over-mapped and pulses under-mapped relative to official area statistics.
  • The paper is not yet peer-reviewed.
