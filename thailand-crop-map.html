MIT Earth Intelligence Lab · AAAI 2024

A national crop map of Thailand from Street View and zero-shot vision-language-model labels

GPT‑4V zero-shot labels of Street View images, with no expert labels in training, supervise a national 10 m map of rice, cassava, maize and sugarcane that reaches 92% overall accuracy, within one point of the expert-supervised map.

J. Laguarta Soler, T. Friedel, S. Wang · Proc. AAAI 38(20), 2024 · first author

PaperarXivCode

My role

I built the system: curating Street View images of crop fields, training the street-view crop classifiers, comparing GPT‑4V zero-shot, expert and web-scraped labels as their supervision, and transferring the resulting field labels to a satellite time-series classifier for the national map.

Problem

Satellite crop-type classifiers need ground labels, and smallholder regions rarely have field surveys or the expert time to annotate at scale. We test whether street-level imagery and zero-shot labels from a vision-language model can replace both as the source of training labels.

Method

GPT‑4V labels the crop in Street View images of fields zero-shot, with 95% accuracy on the street-view test set. A CNN trained on these labels pseudo-labels the remaining images, and its high-confidence geotagged labels supervise a random forest on satellite time series that maps all of Thailand at 10 m. We compare the same pipeline supervised by expert labels and by weak web labels (Google Images, iNaturalist).

Results

Limitations

  • Expert labels are still needed for evaluation; accuracy is measured against expert labels, not field surveys.
  • The map covers a single season and four main crops.

Follow-up work

The India crop map scales the same approach: Gemini 3.1 Pro labels street-level imagery across all 737 districts, with no per-image human annotation.
