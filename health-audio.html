Acoustic biomarkers for COVID‑19 and Alzheimer's screening

Acoustic-biomarker models for COVID‑19 from forced coughs (IEEE OJEMB 2020, 715 citations; AUC 0.97 in the official-test subgroup) and Alzheimer's disease from spontaneous speech (93.8% accuracy on the ADReSS benchmark).

J. Laguarta, F. Hueto, B. Subirana (IEEE OJEMB 2020) · J. Laguarta, B. Subirana (Frontiers in Computer Science 2021) · first author on both

COVID‑19 paperAlzheimer's paper

My role

I co-developed a set of acoustic-biomarker feature extractors for disease pre-screening and transferred them to COVID‑19 detection from forced coughs and Alzheimer's detection from spontaneous speech, with per-patient saliency maps derived from the biomarker outputs for longitudinal tracking. I helped collect the cough data and set up clinical data collaborations with the Catalan health system and Universidad de Guadalajara.

Method

We collected more than 70,000 recordings (about 200,000 coughs, about 2,500 from COVID-positive people) through a public web platform. Each recording is encoded by acoustic-biomarker feature extractors pretrained on separate audio tasks, and a classifier on their combined features makes the prediction.

Limitations

  • A later large study (Coppock et al., Nature Machine Intelligence, 2024, about 67,000 participants) found that, once recruitment bias is controlled, audio COVID‑19 classifiers do not outperform self-reported symptoms; our results were not validated prospectively.
  • The Alzheimer's benchmark is small.
